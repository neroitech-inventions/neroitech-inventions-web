Executive Summary

On October 20, 2025, a significant AWS outage disrupted numerous services globally, beginning at 11:49 PM PDT on October 19 and lasting approximately 15 hours until full resolution at around 6:00 PM EDT on October 20 [AWS Health Dashboard]. This incident, rooted in a DynamoDB DNS resolution failure in the US-EAST-1 region [Amazon Official Statement], affected over 113 AWS services [CNN Business, AWS Health Dashboard], highlighting the fragility of centralized cloud infrastructure [CNN Business].

Key findings from the outage include the resilience of major Layer 1 (L1) blockchains like Ethereum, Solana, and Avalanche, which maintained 100% uptime despite varying levels of AWS dependency [Decrypt, CryptoSlate, Brave New Coin]. The outage also caused specific disruptions in the crypto ecosystem, including MetaMask users reporting zero balances due to Infura outages [Decrypt], and Coinbase experiencing login failures and trading halts [Fortune Crypto, CNBC].

The outage underscored the systemic risk posed by AWS's control over approximately 30% of global cloud infrastructure [Bloomberg, CNBC], and the concentration of Ethereum nodes on AWS, with about 37% hosted on the platform [CryptoSlate]. Technical Analysis

The root cause of the AWS outage was identified as a DynamoDB DNS resolution failure in the US-EAST-1 region, as confirmed by Amazon's official statement [Amazon Official Statement]. This failure led to cascading effects across numerous AWS services, impacting a wide range of applications and services globally [AWS Health Dashboard, CNN Business].

Technical analysis by ThousandEyes revealed that the outage resulted in significant network diagnostics issues, affecting service availability and latency [ThousandEyes]. The concentration of infrastructure on AWS, which controls around 30% of global cloud infrastructure [Bloomberg], amplified the impact of the outage on dependent services.

In the blockchain space, L1 networks like Ethereum, Solana, and Avalanche demonstrated resilience, maintaining 100% uptime despite AWS dependencies ranging from 6% to 35% [Decrypt, CryptoSlate, Brave New Coin]. This resilience can be attributed to their decentralized nature and the distribution of nodes across multiple cloud providers and on-premise setups.

In contrast, L2 networks, which rely more heavily on centralized infrastructure, experienced performance degradation. Base, with a 65% dependency on AWS, saw a 25% reduction in throughput [Decrypt, CryptoSlate]. The outage's impact on these networks was exacerbated by their reliance on Infura, a critical infrastructure provider that experienced outages during the event [Decrypt, CryptoSlate].
